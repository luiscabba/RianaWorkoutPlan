The one rule

Double progression. Chase failure in the 8–12 zone, then let the reps tell you when to change the weight.

Coach Notes
  1. Don't take the barbell lifts to true failure. On squat, deadlift, and overhead press, stop 1–2 reps short — form breaks before the muscle does, and that's where injuries happen. Save all-out failure for machines and accessories.
  2. Ramp up before the big lifts. Before your first working set on squats and deadlifts, do 2–3 light ascending sets to groove the movement. These don't count toward your two working sets.
  3. Rest with intent. ~2–3 min on compounds, ~60–90s on accessories and core. That's what keeps a session inside the 60–90 minute window.
  4. Run it Mon / Wed / Fri with a rest day between. Once recovery feels easy, repeat a day to train 4×/week — but 3×/week is plenty to build real strength as a beginner.
  5. Film the compounds from the side now and then. Form feedback is the highest-leverage thing a new lifter can do.
